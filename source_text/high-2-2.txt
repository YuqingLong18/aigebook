2.2 Learning-Based AI

(3)	Master the five key elements of the basic framework of machine learning (objective, model, algorithm, data, and knowledge), and understand the role of each in building a system.
(4)	Recognize the importance of data in machine learning, and understand how data quality, quantity, and scenario coverage (this term should mean how well the data is representative of all cases in practice, but I can’t think of a better phrase at the moment) affect model training outcomes.

To be able to let machines learn and grow on their own is not only an exciting idea, but also one of the foundational visions of AI, even in the Turing era. As early as 1959, American computer scientist Arthur Samuel designed a program capable of self-learning and called this new approach “machine learning.” However, for a long time, due to the lack of data resources and limited computational power, machine learning remained at the margins of AI research. It wasn’t until the 21st century, with the accumulation of data and advances in computing hardware, that large-scale machine learning became possible. Today, all the astonishing breakthroughs we see in AI are inseparable from machine learning. This section introduces the basic framework and core concepts of machine learning and discusses how machines can use it to achieve (and perhaps even surpass) human-level intelligence.

1.The Importance of Learning
Learning is one of the most important cognitive processes for humans, and something we do throughout lives. From the fetal stage, our brains are already engaged in various learning activities. Research has shown that by the 30th week of pregnancy, the fetal brain can already process and retain certain forms of information, indicating that the central nervous system is ready for learning. After birth, we are constantly learning: we learn to speak like our parents, to walk on our own, to fold paper airplanes from friends, and to master subjects like language and mathematics from teachers. One could go so far as to say that existence itself is a continuous process of learning.

Not only humans need to learn. Animals also learn their survival skills through experience. Interestingly, scientists have found that even plants may also have learning abilities. For example, an article in the journal Scientific Reports describes an experiment in which corn seedlings were “trained” (see Figure 2-11). Researchers placed a light source and a fan on the same side. Gradually, the seedlings “learned” that wind meant the presence of light. When the light source was removed and the fan direction changed, the seedlings still grew toward the fan, inferring that the wind indicated light, based on their learned experience.

2.Machine Learning
In the previous section, we discussed how early AI was dominated by knowledge-based approaches: providing machines with human knowledge and reasoning rules so that they could “think”, much like how a teacher distills key knowledge points and teaches them to students. Those knowledge-based methods achieved great success in tasks like theorem proving and expert systems, but they had obvious shortcomings: First, summarizing all the needed knowledge was tedious and time-consuming. Second, the machine was confined within the pre-programmed boundaries of the knowledge framework, limiting its abilities.  Under a knowledge-based approach, it could never go beyond what humans already knew.

In 1959, American scientist Arthur Samuel formally proposed the concept of machine learning and demonstrated it in a checkers game (see Figure 2-12). In the experiment, Samuel only gave the machine the game rules, some general strategies, and a few parameters to control the moves. He found that by adjusting these parameters, the machine could outperform its programmer himself in 8 to 10 hours of learning.

After that, machine learning developed rapidly. People proposed models like Bayesian networks and artificial neural networks (see Figure 2-13), significantly enhancing the learning capabilities of machines. Especially since the 21st century, with the surge in data availability and computing power, machines have been able to learn more and, in so doing, become more powerful, directly fueling new waves of AI breakthroughs.

3.The Basic Framework of Machine Learning
Machine learning is a method that uses appropriate algorithms to learn from data in order to improve initial models designed with prior knowledge, thereby enabling the system to achieve its goals more effectively.

In contrast to knowledge-based approaches, which require explicitly programming every detail of a machine’s behavior, machine learning does not specify how a task should be done. Instead, it defines a goal and lets the machine independently figure out how to achieve the goal through a process of trial and error.
The basic framework of machine learning includes five components: objective, model, algorithm, data, and knowledge (see Figure 2-14).

(1) Objective
First, a learning objective must be defined so that the machine knows which direction to pursue. Typical goals include improving classification accuracy, making better predictions, or generating more realistic data.

Objectives must be expressed in strict mathematical form—such as classification error or prediction error. These mathematical formulations are often called loss functions. The lower the loss, the better the performance.

For example, in a classification task, we may observe the proportion of samples that are correctly classified. The higher this proportion, the better. That proportion is the learning objective. Likewise, in a house price prediction task, we may measure how close the predicted value is to the actual price. The smaller the difference (absolute error), the better. That error, much like the earlier proportion, becomes the learning objective.

(2) Model
Any form of learning requires a “learner”, just as humans need a brain (a “thinker” if you will) to learn. In essence, this learner is a data structure that can be updated and refined over time to accumulate learning results. This learner is called a model. A model might be a simple production system or a more complex structure like a Bayesian network or artificial neural network.

A production system, for instance, may include a statement like “if the sky is cloudy and humidity is high, then it will rain.” These rules can be derived from data, and their order or weighting can be adjusted based on observations. In Bayesian networks, what is learned are the probabilistic relationships between variables. In artificial neural networks, it is the connection weights between neurons that are adjusted through learning. Each model has its own advantages and limitations. No single model is always superior (a principle known as the No Free Lunch Theorem).

(3) Algorithm
An algorithm is the computational process used to update a model. For example, it may adjust the connection weights in a neural network to improve its ability to recognize traffic lights, or tweak the probability functions in a Bayesian network to more accurately model stock market influences.

Different models require different learning methods. Most modern models use numerical optimization techniques to minimize loss functions. For complex problems, genetic algorithms, which mimic the principles of biological evolution, can also be used.

(4) Data
Data is the “nutrition” of machine learning and the raw material from which knowledge is extracted. The goal of machine learning is to gather the knowledge hidden in scattered data and store it in models. To train a high-quality machine learning model, data must be carefully selected. For the purposes of machine learning, data should meet the following requirements: High quality, especially when labeled by humans (the labels must be accurate); Sufficient in quantity (too little data makes it hard to train a good model); Broad in coverage (for example, a face recognition dataset should include faces under various lighting conditions and from different angles).

With the development of large language models, these data requirements have grown. For example, GPT-3.5 was trained on 45TB of text data. Managing such vast data involves cleaning low-quality samples, balancing data sources, and ensuring efficient learning. Some scientists predict that AI will soon exhaust all of humanity’s available data. How to handle this looming “data drought” is a major challenge on the horizon.

(5) Knowledge
Every machine learning system is designed for a specific task, and each task comes with its own domain-specific constraints. These include the type of data, its volume, model parameter ranges, and deployment conditions.

These constraints are referred to as prior knowledge. Designers must fully utilize such knowledge to build effective machine learning systems.

As AI technology advances and consumes more human-generated data, prior knowledge becomes increasingly valuable. It can guide and constrain the learning process, making it easier for machines to extract meaningful information from data.

4.Advantages of Machine Learning
AI is a scientific discipline aimed at simulating human-like intelligence. There are many ways to achieve this goal, and machine learning is one of the most important. It enables intelligence through autonomous learning from data.

Compared to traditional knowledge-based AI approaches, machine learning has the potential to transcend human knowledge limits, discover new patterns unknown to humans, explore novel paths to achieve goals, and even attain “superhuman” intelligence. The impressive capabilities of today’s AI (along with the oft-discussed risks) are largely due to machine learning. Only self-learning machines have the potential to truly surpass their creators and develop powerful, but unpredictable capabilities.

Discussion: Why can machines potentially surpass humans through learning, while traditional knowledge-based methods struggle to do so?

Hint: In traditional approaches, humans must first summarize knowledge and then pass it on to machines, so the machine’s upper limit is constrained by what humans already know. But in machine learning, both humans and machines begin on equal footing—both learn from data. However, they each have unique strengths: machines are faster and have better memory; humans have stronger intuition and deeper insight. Both can discover new knowledge from data, but what they discover might differ. This gives machines the potential to surpass humans, learning things that humans haven’t (or can’t). Of course, the reverse is also true: machines won’t necessarily learn everything humans have the potential to.

5. A Machine Learning Example: Distinguishing Apples from Oranges
Objective: Classify apples and oranges. Mathematically, the goal is to maximize classification accuracy or minimize error rate.
Knowledge: Large and red is likely an apple; smaller and more orange-colored is likely an orange.
Model: Y = a × color + b × size. Here, a and b are model parameters to be adjusted during learning.
Data: We collect apples and oranges, observe their color and size, and assign labels: apple (T = 1), orange (T = 0).
Algorithm: Gradually adjust the values of a and b so that the predicted output Y approaches the label T.

Once learning is complete, we have a model that can classify apples and oranges. In Figure 2-15, the blue line represents the model's decision boundary (above it are apples, below it are oranges). For a new sample, we simply check which side of the line it falls on to determine whether it’s an apple or an orange.

Section Summary
Machine learning is the core idea and foundational method of modern AI. It uses algorithms to autonomously learn from data, acquiring the skills needed to complete target tasks. The five key elements of machine learning are: objective, model, algorithm, data, and knowledge. With these components, machines can independently learn from data, acquire knowledge that humans have not yet discovered, and explore new ways to reach goals.  These three abilities demonstrate intelligence that has the potential to surpass human capabilities. Machine learning not only overcomes the limitations of traditional knowledge-based AI methods but also lays the foundation for the flourishing of AI today.
